Limova Review (2026): Pricing, Agents and What It Does

Limova puts a first name on every AI agent it sells. Charming branding, real questions underneath it, and one detail the marketing does not lead with.

Read this part if nothing elseThis is a French product for French businesses, and that is the single most useful thing to know before you sign up. The site, the agent pages, the offers page and the support line are all in French, so if you are shopping from outside that market the friction starts before the free trial does. What you get is a catalogue of named agents split by job, phone, marketing, SEO, sales, accounting, legal and recruitment, plus a general assistant, which is a genuinely clearer way to sell this than another blank chat box. The pitch, translated

The headline on the front page promises autonomous AI agents working for your business, with the supporting line about freeing yourself from repetitive tasks so you can get back to growing the company. Underneath that sits a demo prompt about generating five Instagram visuals for the week, which tells you the intended buyer: a small business owner or a marketing lead, not an engineering team. Two buttons dominate the fold, a free trial and a demo booking, and a French phone number sits in the header next to them. It is a conventional software as a service layout executed cleanly, and the copy stays away from technical claims, which is either restraint or a gap depending on what you were hoping to find.

One agent per job, with a name attached

The navigation lists eight agents and a premium tier. Tom handles phone calls, John covers marketing, Lou does SEO, Elio is the sales agent, Manue takes accounting, Julia covers legal, Rony works on recruitment, and Charly is the general assistant, with Charly Plus sold separately. Each has its own page and its own portrait. Giving a persona to a narrow workflow is a sales decision more than a technical one, but it works: a buyer who cannot evaluate a model can absolutely decide whether they want a recruitment assistant. What the homepage does not do is describe how any of these agents connect to your existing systems, which is the first question a serious buyer should ask on the demo call.

Proof, promo codes and the fine print

Two kinds of social proof appear above the fold. The site displays a star rating of 4.9 out of 5 with more than a thousand reviews, and a strip of French media logos under a heading that translates as they are talking about us, including Le Figaro, TF1, BFM Business, LCI and W9. Both are claims made on the vendor's own site, and neither has been verified here. A promotional banner runs across the top offering ten percent off monthly and twenty-five percent off annual with named codes, which suggests discounting is routine rather than exceptional. If you are comparing quotes, that banner is worth remembering before you accept a list price.

What the homepage will not tell you

No prices appear on the front page. Everything numeric lives behind the offers page, and the crawl used for this review did not include it, so this page quotes no figures at all rather than guessing at them. There is also no published documentation of integrations, data handling or where the underlying models run, at least not from the entry page. For an accounting or legal agent that is not a small omission, it is the whole risk assessment. Ask about data residency and retention on the demo call, get the answer in writing, and treat anything you read on a third-party review site, including this one, as a starting point rather than a source.

Worth knowing before the trial

French first, everywhere

The site, the agent pages, the offers page and the support number are all French. If your team does not operate in French, budget for that friction before you evaluate anything else about the product.

Nine tiles, not one chatbox

Eight named agents by function plus a premium general assistant. Buying by job title rather than by model is easier to reason about, and it also makes it obvious when a job you need is missing.

Prices live behind a click

Nothing numeric sits on the homepage. The offers page holds the plans, and a standing discount banner suggests the list price is negotiable more often than not.

How to evaluate it without wasting a week

  1. Pick one agent, not eight
    Choose the single function where repetitive work hurts most, usually SEO or sales follow-up, and evaluate only that agent page. A broad trial produces a vague opinion.
  2. Read the offers page first
    Open the pricing page before the trial so you know which tier you are actually testing. Note the discount codes in the banner while they are live.
  3. Ask the integration question early
    On the demo call, ask how the agent reaches your CRM, inbox or site, and what happens to the data afterwards. Get it in writing, not in a call summary.
  4. Run one real task end to end
    Give it a task you would otherwise pay someone to do, then compare the output against what that person produces. Anything less is a feature tour.

How much does Limova cost?

The homepage does not publish figures, so this page will not invent them. Plans live on the offers page, and a banner at the top of the site advertises percentage discounts on monthly and annual billing with named codes. Check both before assuming the list price is the price.

Is there an English version?

Nothing in the crawled homepage suggests one. The navigation, agent names, calls to action and the support phone number are all French, and the phone number is a French landline. Treat English support as something to confirm before purchase rather than assume.

They are agent personas, one per business function. Tom is the phone agent, Lou handles SEO, Charly is the general assistant with a Charly Plus tier sold separately, and the rest cover marketing, sales, accounting, legal and recruitment. Each has its own page on the site.
